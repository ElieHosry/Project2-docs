Design Goals Pool — Games (benchmark-anchored)

Status: live — the Games category's Design pool (benchmark-anchored, adopted July 2026). This is the Design goal set for the Games category. The other 9 categories continue to use the base Design Goals Pool and its Category adaptations. UI/UX goals are excluded here — every category, Games included, takes them from the shared UI/UX Goals Pool; for Games, those UI/UX goals are graded by the Design instructor (the UI/UX instructor grades them for the other 9 categories).

Provenance: distilled from AdditionalContext/Mobile_Game_Benchmark_Selection_Criteria.docx (curation criteria) and AdditionalContext/Mobile_Game_Gamification_Benchmark_Single_List.xlsx (20-title benchmark library). Module conventions: up to 3 goals per designer per milestone, shared across the Design and UI/UX pools — the Design common is D1 ⭐ and the rest are picks; quality ladders Fail → Minimum Expectation → Exceptional (Fail = below the Minimum Expectation, goal not met); mobile-first target (Pixel 8a/9a and/or mobile web); AI for ideation only (annotated, never shipped).

How this pool is derived

The base Design pool derives its goals from the old module rubrics. This Games pool re-derives them from a curated benchmark library of shipped mobile games and gamified apps, so every goal is anchored to real professional exemplars and graded against their production bar. The guiding principle from the criteria doc governs the whole pool:

Benchmarks are to inspire production quality, design thinking and appropriate scope — not to be cloned. Students reinterpret design principles for their own project.

Two goals unique to this pool fall out of the new material: a Benchmark Study & Reinterpretation research goal (M1) and a Monetization & Scope Integrity goal (M2), reflecting the library's clean-monetization and appropriate-scope selection criteria.

Selection criteria (from the criteria doc — the bar every benchmark cleared)

  1. Appropriate project scope — recreatable as a small-scale, highly-polished working prototype by one multidisciplinary team over two trimesters.
  2. Commercial success / recognition — market popularity, awards, retention or influence.
  3. Clean monetization — premium, cosmetics, optional non-pay-to-win passes; excludes forced ads and pay-to-win.
  4. Educational value — demonstrates one or more strong production/design disciplines.
  5. Production quality — professional polish, readability, animation, feedback, art direction.
  6. Genre representation — spans many mobile genres.
  7. Design diversity — each exposes a distinct design challenge.
  8. Industry relevance — contemporary trends, reinterpreted not copied.
  9. Local industry awareness — includes Singapore-developed titles.
  10. Beyond games — includes gamified apps (health, education, productivity, commerce).

Design v2 — Games · Milestone 1 (Research phase)

  1. ⭐ Features Design Document (benchmark-informed) — the design backbone, calibrated against a chosen primary benchmark.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: 6–8 page FDD (concept, core loop, mechanics, USP, controls incl. touch, progression overview, references) that names a primary benchmark from the library and 2 secondary ones, states how the project reinterprets (not clones) their principles, and calibrates scope to a declared ★ rating; the team's problem statement; feasibility against the CS engine plan; signed off by all members.
    • Prerequisites: None — foundation of the design track.
    • Feeds into: Every goal in the pool; the wk4 pitch; CS M1 G2; M2 D1 ⭐.
    • Benchmarks: the team's chosen primary + any 2 from the library at or below the target ★ scope.
    • Quality ladder: Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard). Minimum Expectation = reinterpretation is explicit (what we take / what we change) and scope matches the ★ bar. Exceptional = a genuinely distinct USP that advances on its benchmarks.
  2. Core Gameplay Loop Prototype — a playable proof of the 30-second loop; the design risk-killer.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: Playable prototype of the core loop with no critical crashes; title/help screen; readme (controls, how to play); touch controls to industry standard.
    • Recommended prototype software: a commercial game engine (e.g., Unity) — a playable game prototype has to convey real-time feel and timing that Figma can't; Figma suits only static menu/UI mockups (a UI/UX-pool concern). Prototyping only — the shipped build is the team's own C++ engine.
    • Prerequisites: D1 ⭐.
    • Feeds into: M1 playtests; the wk9 checkpoint; the CS team's M2 target reference.
    • Benchmarks: Crossy Road (one-input loop, ★1), Fruit Ninja (tactile loop), Alto's Odyssey (flow).
    • Quality ladder: Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard). Minimum Expectation = industry-standard minimal controls with audio-visual feedback that reads. Exceptional = the USP is already felt in play, not just described.
  3. Benchmark Study & Reinterpretation — structured teardown of the library, turned into design decisions (v2-specific).
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: Teardown of ≥3 benchmarks (ideally across groups A/B/C) — for each: the discipline it exemplifies, why it works, what is in-scope to reinterpret and what to deliberately leave out; findings written back into the FDD as concrete decisions (not a book report).
    • Prerequisites: None — runs from wk3 alongside team formation.
    • Feeds into: D1 ⭐ (decisions), the systems/level/narrative picks below.
    • Benchmarks: at least one Singapore title (group B) is required, to meet the local-awareness criterion.
    • Quality ladder: Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard). Minimum Expectation = each teardown changed a documented design decision. Exceptional = original synthesis across references that yields a defensible, distinctive direction.
  4. Game Feel & Touch Interaction Specification — the tactile rules that make the mobile input responsive.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: Touch-first input scheme (zones, gestures) + game-feel spec (input buffering, acceleration curves, forgiveness, hit-stop, feedback timing) authored as tunable parameters for the CS team.
    • Prerequisites: D1 ⭐ (mechanics to map).
    • Feeds into: CS M1 G1 ⭐ (input requirements); M2 juice pass.
    • Benchmarks: Fruit Ninja (gesture feedback), Alto's Odyssey (one-touch flow), Crossy Road (tap precision).
    • Quality ladder: Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard). Minimum Expectation = feel parameters specified as data. Exceptional = feel validated in the prototype with test evidence.
  5. Level & Progression Design — the difficulty curve and the levels that teach without a manual.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: Level design doc: paper maps/greyboxes, difficulty & pacing curve, teach-test-twist beat structure, progression/unlock rules; authored to fit the CS level-editor data format.
    • Prerequisites: D1 ⭐.
    • Feeds into: M2 working prototype; CS M2 G2 ⭐ (editor content).
    • Benchmarks: Kingdom Rush (wave/level pacing), Vampire Survivors (power curve), Cat Quest (scoped RPG progression).
    • Quality ladder: Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard). Minimum Expectation = levels teach mechanics without text. Exceptional = paper-playtested with documented iterations.
  6. Systems & Economy Design — every resource and system has a source, a sink and a purpose.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: System diagram of all resources/mechanics and their interlocks; spreadsheet balance model with tunable variables; a "no purposeless system" audit against the FDD.
    • Prerequisites: D1 ⭐.
    • Feeds into: M2 tuning; CS M1 G2 ⭐ (which values are data-driven).
    • Benchmarks: Mini Metro & Mini Motorways (resource/flow systems from few rules), Cuisineer (interlocking management systems), The Signal State (systems-as-puzzle).
    • Quality ladder: Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard). Minimum Expectation = interlocks explicit; the model answers "what if" questions. Exceptional = predictions checked against prototype play data.
  7. Narrative & Decision Design — story and meaningful choice, delivered through systems.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: Theme/tone definition, concise world/character bible, narrative beats for the working prototype, and a plan for in-game delivery (ambient, dialogue, decision, cutscene); where relevant, a branching/decision map.
    • Prerequisites: D1 ⭐.
    • Feeds into: M2 narrative presentation; the Art track's direction (or the Art POC conditional for no-Art teams).
    • Benchmarks: Reigns (branching decisions from one verb), Masquerada (dialogue & tactical presentation), Does Not Commute (narrative through systems).
    • Quality ladder: Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard). Minimum Expectation = narrative reinforces mechanics (ludonarrative fit). Exceptional = a distinctive voice/world that strengthens the USP.
  8. Puzzle & Mechanic Design — depth from a small, elegant rule set (for puzzle/mechanic-led projects).
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: Core mechanic definition, a catalogue of puzzle/situation types it generates, a difficulty ramp, and 5–8 designed puzzles/scenarios authored to the CS data format.
    • Prerequisites: D1 ⭐.
    • Feeds into: M2 working prototype; CS content/editor goals.
    • Benchmarks: Monument Valley (elegant spatial puzzles), The Signal State (systems interface puzzles), Cubix (mechanic polish from simplicity).
    • Quality ladder: Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard). Minimum Expectation = difficulty ramps cleanly; puzzles teach themselves. Exceptional = the mechanic yields surprising, elegant situations (paper-tested).
  9. Engagement & Retention Loop Design — session, streak and reward loops that respect the player (gamified-apps lens).
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: Design of the session loop and any meta/retention loop (daily goals, streaks, rewards, progression), with an explicit ethics line: engagement must serve the player's goal, never dark-pattern or pay-to-win (ties to the clean-monetization criterion).
    • Prerequisites: D1 ⭐; pairs with D6 (systems).
    • Feeds into: M2 progression & retention tuning; CS analytics/Firebase goals.
    • Benchmarks: Duolingo (streaks/feedback), Forest (focus-session loop), Healthy 365 (goals & rewards), Habitica (RPG-ified tasks) — with Shopee cited as the boundary case (event loops that must NOT become manipulative).
    • Quality ladder: Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard). Minimum Expectation = retention mechanics justified against player value, not just time-on-app. Exceptional = loop validated with users and shown to aid the intended goal.

Conditional (mandatory pick for teams with NO Art student): Art Proof of Concept — max-5-page Art POC (references/concept art, style guide for all elements, mood board); AI ideation annotated, never shipped. Same as v1 D11 M1.

Design v2 — Games · Milestone 2 (Iterated Prototype phase)

Milestone-2 benchmark = a working prototype: a small-scale game at a very high level of polish. Content volume scales to the team — some deliver more, some less — so this is not framed as a fixed "vertical slice"; by M2 it is the team's full-content prototype. Polish is the bar, not breadth.

  1. ⭐ Features Design Iteration & Showcase Readiness — the FDD re-cut from evidence, scoped to a demoable working prototype.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: Updated FDD documenting what changed since M1 and why (playtest/benchmark evidence), the final working-prototype scope, and a showcase plan; re-checked against the primary benchmark's production bar.
    • Prerequisites: M1 D1 ⭐ and the M1 playtests.
    • Feeds into: The trimester showcase; every M2 pick.
    • Benchmarks: the primary benchmark, re-examined for the specific working prototype being shipped.
    • Quality ladder: Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard). Minimum Expectation = changes traced to evidence; scope realistic for the prototype. Exceptional = a tightened, demo-ready design that visibly closes the gap to its benchmark.
  2. Working Prototype — Full-Content Build — a small-scale game at a very high level of polish; content volume scales to the team (some deliver more, some less) — polish, not breadth, is the bar.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: A small-scale but highly polished **working prototype** — the complete core experience with success/failure conditions and easy retry/continue, authored in the team's editor; content volume scales to the team (polish, not breadth, is the bar); an approved waiver applies to endless/no-fail designs.
    • Recommended prototype software: the team's own C++ engine (the showcase build) — by M2 the M1 Unity prototype has served its purpose as the design reference; no external prototyping tool here.
    • Prerequisites: M1 D5 or D8; CS M2 G2 ⭐ (editor).
    • Feeds into: The showcase demo; T2 full-level production.
    • Benchmarks: Kingdom Rush (a self-contained level), Monument Valley (a polished chapter), Cubix (tight scope).
    • Quality ladder: Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard). Minimum Expectation = paced, teaching, polished. Exceptional = a full-content prototype — every mechanic represented at showcase-level polish.
  3. Systems Balance & Tuning — Data-Driven — turn the M1 model into a tuned game via the team's own data files.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: Tuning passes on the real build using the CS data-driven architecture (no code changes); tuning journal (what/why/result); final balance state for showcase.
    • Prerequisites: M1 D6; CS M1 G2 ⭐.
    • Feeds into: Showcase play quality.
    • Benchmarks: Mini Metro (difficulty from few knobs), Vampire Survivors (power/enemy curve).
    • Quality ladder: Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard). Minimum Expectation = playtest-informed decisions. Exceptional = telemetry-assisted tuning.
  4. Game Feel & Feedback Pass ("Juice") — every action answers the player instantly.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: Feedback spec + implementation with CS: hit reactions, particles, screen effects, audio cues on meaningful interactions, serving the core mechanic.
    • Prerequisites: M1 D4; CS graphics/animation goals.
    • Feeds into: The showcase's perceived quality.
    • Benchmarks: Alto's Odyssey (understated polish), Fruit Ninja (punchy feedback), Vampire Survivors (readable chaos).
    • Quality ladder: Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard). Minimum Expectation = cues meaningful and legible, not noise. Exceptional = feedback is a signature of the game.
  5. Playtesting & Iteration Program — test, triage, change, re-test.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: ≥1 structured M2 session with target-player testers, metrics (task success, time, ratings), a triaged findings backlog, and documented before/after changes.
    • Prerequisites: M1 D3/benchmark method; M2 builds.
    • Feeds into: D1 ⭐ (evidence), D3 (tuning data).
    • Benchmarks: approach — how the benchmarks' clarity was achieved through iteration (design method, not a title to copy).
    • Quality ladder: Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard). Minimum Expectation = changes traceable to findings. Exceptional = multiple sessions with measurable improvement.
  6. Progression & Retention Tuning — the meta-loop tuned for genuine, healthy engagement.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: Tuning of the progression/retention loop from M2 playtests (pacing of rewards, streaks, unlocks), with the ethics line upheld (player value over time-on-app).
    • Prerequisites: M1 D9 (or D5 progression); CS data-driven values.
    • Feeds into: Showcase longevity story; T2 live-ops thinking.
    • Benchmarks: Vampire Survivors (unlock pacing), Duolingo (streak cadence), Forest (intrinsic reward).
    • Quality ladder: Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard). Minimum Expectation = pacing justified by playtests. Exceptional = retention shown to serve the player's goal, evidenced.
  7. Narrative Presentation & Delivery — context delivered in-game with craft.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: Primitive cutscenes or ambient/decision storytelling answering "who am I / where am I / what must I do", integrated in the build with dynamic presentation.
    • Prerequisites: M1 D7; art assets from the Art track or the Art conditional.
    • Feeds into: Showcase storytelling; T2 full narrative.
    • Benchmarks: Reigns (economical delivery), Masquerada (presentation quality), Does Not Commute (story via systems).
    • Quality ladder: Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard). Minimum Expectation = dynamic presentation (not static text walls). Exceptional = presentation quality that elevates the whole product.
  8. Monetization & Scope Integrity Design — a clean, honest model and a scope that ships (v2-specific).
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: A monetization design that fits the module's clean-monetization rule (premium, cosmetics, optional non-pay-to-win passes; NO forced ads, NO pay-to-win) with a short rationale, plus a scope-integrity statement mapping the working prototype to the declared ★ rating and cutting anything that endangers a demoable showcase.
    • Prerequisites: M1 D1 ⭐ and D9 if picked.
    • Feeds into: Showcase framing; the go/no-go on stretch features.
    • Benchmarks: Monument Valley / Crossy Road / Vampire Survivors (clean models); Shopee as the boundary case to argue *against* dark patterns.
    • Quality ladder: Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard). Minimum Expectation = model justified against player value; scope demonstrably demoable. Exceptional = a model and scope a real studio could defend publicly.
  9. Art Direction & Visual Identity Brief — the design-side visual direction that briefs the Art track.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: A visual-identity brief (mood, palette rationale, references, tone) that the Art track executes; distinct from UI work (which lives in the UI/UX pool). For no-Art teams this feeds the Art conditional.
    • Prerequisites: M1 D1 ⭐, D7.
    • Feeds into: Art track direction; showcase coherence.
    • Benchmarks: Masquerada (art direction), Alto's Odyssey (atmosphere), Monument Valley (visual identity).
    • Quality ladder: Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard). Minimum Expectation = direction distinctive and applied consistently. Exceptional = a professional-grade identity that reads instantly.

Conditional (mandatory pick for teams with NO Art student): Art Implementation Quality — all implemented art aligned to the Art POC (no placeholders), readable, consistently scaled; all shipped art team-made. Same as v1 D11 M2.
