Design Goals Pool

Status: approved by the Design instructor (July 2026). Distilled from the previous module's Designers-Rubrics (M1–M3) and the New Preliminary Design timeline. The base pool below is the canonical reference for the Design track (Games-flavored wording); the Category adaptations section maps it onto the other 9 categories. The Games category now uses its own benchmark-anchored pool — see Games Design Goals (adopted July 2026); the base below is the shared skeleton the adaptations reference, not a category's live pool. Amendment (July 9): the audio picks (former M1 D9, M2 D10) migrated to the TEAM Goals; the pools below are renumbered. Amendment (July 26, approved by the Design and UI/UX instructors): all UI/UX goals (the TU commons, the UX/accessibility/motion/onboarding/mobile-UX picks, and the former M2 D1 ⭐) moved OUT to the dedicated UI/UX Goals Pool, graded by the UI/UX instructor (the Games category excepted — its UI/UX goals are graded by the Design instructor); M2 D1 ⭐ here was re-founded as Features Design Iteration & Showcase Readiness. Design capacity remains up to 3 goals per Design student per milestone, counted across both pools.

How Design goals work

Each team has 1–2 Design students (minimum 1, mandated). Unlike the CS track (one 40 h goal per student per milestone), Design goals are lighter, deliverable-sized units: each Design student takes up to 3 goals per milestone (three Design goals ≈ 1.5 CS goals in effort). Every team's Design track carries the common D1 ⭐ per milestone (the mandated designer owns it); the UI/UX commons live in the UI/UX Goals Pool and are also owned by Design students. Remaining slots are pool picks (all picks with instructor approval).

Grading: every goal on this page is graded by the Design instructor; all UI/UX goals (separate pool) are graded by the UI/UX instructor — except the Games category, whose UI/UX goals the Design instructor grades. All grades affect the Design students only.

Default picks: the mandated designer's M1 pick is normally D2 Core-Loop Interactive Prototype — this preserves the old module's compulsory Features DD + prototype pair on every team.

Conditional rule: pool goals marked (conditional) are mandatory picks for teams with no Art student. Since this consumes a pick, teams with 0 artists are strongly advised to carry 2 designers; a solo-designer/0-artist team must ask the instructor which conditional bends (in that case only, D1 ⭐ absorbs a minimal core-loop sketch in place of D2).

Sizing: Design goals are sized by deliverable + quality tier, not by a strict 40-hour estimate. Every goal carries a Quality ladder (Fail → Minimum Expectation → Exceptional) distilled from the old rubric tiers. Minimum Expectation is the pass floor; work below it is Fail; the top rung raises the grade, not the deadline.

Numbering: D1 ⭐ = the common; D2, D3, … = pool picks. Numbers of moved goals are retired in place (tombstone rows) so existing references stay valid; the UI/UX pool uses fresh U-numbers. Pools are extensible like the CS pools — instructor-approved goals from new proposals are appended at the end.

Cross-track dependencies: several goals reference CS goals (G1–G3 ⭐, #N) from the Games pools — Design deliverables feed the CS data-driven architecture and editors, and consume the CS prototype builds.

Not in this pool: audio direction/production lives in the TEAM Goals — Audio (TA1 ⭐ Overall Audio Experience + TA2 ⭐ Audio Engine Functionality — both mandatory for every team each milestone, shared grade, graded by the Audio instructor).

Design — Base disciplines (canonical reference)

Milestone 1 (Research phase)

  • D1 ⭐ Features Design Document — the design backbone: concept, core loop, mechanics, USP, scope sign-off.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: 6–8 page Features DD (core mechanics, objective, theme, genre, core game loop, control mapping incl. touch, level/progression overview, references) + the team's problem statement + a feasibility check against the CS engine feature plan, signed off by all members + a trimester planning timeline. (Solo-designer/0-artist teams only: may absorb a minimal core-loop sketch in place of D2 — see the conditional rule above.)
    • Prerequisites: None — foundation of the design track.
    • Feeds into: Every goal in both tracks; the wk4 product pitch; CS M1 G2 (what the core objects ARE); M2 D1 ⭐.
    • Quality ladder: Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard). Minimum Expectation = systems interlock with no purposeless mechanics, depth over volume, clean visuals-supported writing. Exceptional = genuinely unique USP that moves the genre forward.
  • TU1 (retired) — moved: now U1 ⭐ in the UI/UX Goals Pool (UI/UX-instructor graded).
  • D2 Core-Loop Interactive Prototype — playable proof of the core mechanics in Figma or a commercial engine; the design risk killer.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: Playable prototype without critical crashes; title/help screen listing controls; readme (controls, how to play, feature list); touch controls follow industry standards.
    • Prerequisites: D1 ⭐ (core loop defined).
    • Feeds into: M1 D9 playtests; the wk9 prototype checkpoint; CS team's target reference for M2.
    • Quality ladder: Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard). Minimum Expectation = industry-standard minimal controls with audio-visual feedback that explains the moment. Exceptional = the USP is already felt in play, not just described.
  • D3 User Research, Personas & Competitive Analysis — know the player and the genre before betting a trimester on them.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: Competitor/genre teardown (3+ titles), target-player personas, research questions with method and findings; implications written back into the Features DD.
    • Prerequisites: None — runs from wk3 alongside team formation.
    • Feeds into: D1 ⭐ (audience-fit decisions), M1 D4 (journey-driven flows), M2 D5 (recruiting playtesters that match personas).
    • Quality ladder: Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard). Minimum Expectation = findings visibly changed design decisions (documented before/after). Exceptional = original research (surveys/interviews) with actionable insight beyond genre convention.
  • D4 — moved: now U2 UX Flows & Wireframes in the UI/UX Goals Pool.
  • D5 Control Mapping & Game Feel Specification — touch-first input design and the feel rules that make it responsive.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: Input schemes for the mobile target (touch zones, gestures) plus keyboard/controller dev fallbacks; game-feel spec (buffering, acceleration curves, forgiveness mechanics) as tunable parameters.
    • Prerequisites: D1 ⭐ (mechanics to map).
    • Feeds into: CS M1 G1 ⭐ (input system requirements), M2 D8 (juice pass), M2 D10 (ergonomics).
    • Quality ladder: Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard). Minimum Expectation = feel parameters specified as data the CS team can tune. Exceptional = feel validated in the prototype with test evidence.
  • D6 Level & Progression Design — the difficulty curve and the levels that teach the game without a manual.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: Level design document: paper maps/greyboxes, difficulty and pacing curve, teach-test-twist beat structure, progression rules; authored to fit the CS level-editor data format.
    • Prerequisites: D1 ⭐ (mechanics and progression frame).
    • Feeds into: M2 D6 (the built level), CS M2 G2 ⭐ (editor content requirements).
    • Quality ladder: Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard). Minimum Expectation = levels teach mechanics without text. Exceptional = paper-playtested with documented iterations.
  • D7 Game Economy & Systems Balance Model — every resource has a source, a sink and a reason to exist.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: System diagram of all resources/mechanics and their interlocks; spreadsheet balance model with tunable variables; "no purposeless system" audit of the Features DD.
    • Prerequisites: D1 ⭐ (systems defined).
    • Feeds into: M2 D7 (tuning), CS M1 G2 ⭐ (which values are data-driven).
    • Quality ladder: Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard). Minimum Expectation = interlocks explicit; model answers "what if" questions. Exceptional = model predictions checked against prototype play data.
  • D8 Narrative, Theme & World Concept — who am I, where am I, what must I do — answered with craft.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: Theme and tone definition, world/character bible (concise), narrative beats for the vertical slice, plan for in-game delivery (ambient, dialogue, cutscene).
    • Prerequisites: D1 ⭐ (theme/genre frame).
    • Feeds into: M2 D9 (cutscene presentation), Art track's art direction (or M1 D11 for no-Art teams).
    • Quality ladder: Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard). Minimum Expectation = narrative reinforces mechanics (ludonarrative fit). Exceptional = distinctive voice/world that strengthens the USP.
  • D9 Playtest Plan & First Playtests — structured observation, not "my friends liked it".
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: Playtest protocol (goals, tasks, observation sheet, consent), at least 3 sessions run on the M1 prototype, findings log with severity ratings and design responses.
    • Prerequisites: M1 D2 (something playable) or D1 ⭐'s core-loop sketch.
    • Feeds into: M2 D5 (the full program), M2 D1 ⭐ (validation evidence).
    • Quality ladder: Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard). Minimum Expectation = findings triaged and visibly acted on. Exceptional = quantitative + qualitative methods combined.
  • D10 — moved: now U3 Accessibility & Readability in the UI/UX Goals Pool.
  • D11 Art Proof of Concept (conditional — mandatory pick for teams with NO Art student) — the design-owned substitute for the Art track's M1.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: Max-5-page Art POC (PDF): art references/concept art for characters or key objects, environments, menus/HUD; an art style guide covering ALL game elements; a UI style guide (fonts, themes, palettes, mood board). AI use for ideation allowed but every AI image annotated with the tool; AI assets may NOT ship in the product.
    • Prerequisites: D1 ⭐ (theme/genre), M1 D8 if picked.
    • Feeds into: M2 D11 (implementation quality), CS art pipeline goals.
    • Quality ladder: Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard). Minimum Expectation = style guides give clear direction for all elements. Exceptional = original team-made mockups near industry quality.

Milestone 2 (Iterated Prototype phase)

  • D1 ⭐ Features Design Iteration & Showcase Readiness — the design capstone: the Features Design Document proven and iterated against reality.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: Updated Features Design Document with documented iteration: what changed since M1 and why (traced to playtests/telemetry), the sharpened USP, final content and balance sign-off for the showcase build, and the designed demo flow (what the audience experiences, in what order).
    • Prerequisites: M1 D1 ⭐; D5/D7 tuning work where picked; the UI/UX pool's U2 ⭐ covers the UI-polish half separately.
    • Feeds into: The showcase build's content; TP1 ⭐ (the demo flow is presented there).
    • Quality ladder: Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard). Minimum Expectation = every major change traced to playtest evidence. Exceptional = iteration demonstrably improved a measured outcome (completion, clarity, fun rating).
  • TU1 (retired) — moved: now U1 ⭐ in the UI/UX Goals Pool.
  • D2 Visual Branding & Style Guide — logo, typography, palette: one identity across game, UI and store presence.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: Logo/wordmark, typography system (max 3 fonts: headings, in-game, small text — policy-compliant), color palette, applied consistently across menus/HUD/marketing shots; style guide document.
    • Prerequisites: M1 D1 ⭐ (theme); M1 D11 or Art track (art direction).
    • Feeds into: M2 D1 ⭐ (polished UI uses it), the showcase presentation.
    • Quality ladder: Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard). Minimum Expectation = identity distinctive and applied everywhere. Exceptional = professional-grade brand system.
  • D3 — moved: now U3 Menu & HUD Motion Design in the UI/UX Goals Pool.
  • D4 — moved: now U4 Onboarding & Tutorial Design in the UI/UX Goals Pool.
  • D5 Playtesting & Iteration Program — the M2 engine of quality: test, triage, change, re-test.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: At least 1 structured session in M2 with persona-matched testers, metrics (task success, time, subjective ratings), a triaged findings backlog, and documented before/after changes.
    • Prerequisites: M1 D9 (protocol) or equivalent; M2 builds.
    • Feeds into: M2 D1 ⭐ (validation evidence), M2 D7 (tuning data).
    • Quality ladder: Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard). Minimum Expectation = changes traceable to findings. Exceptional = multiple sessions with measurable improvement across iterations.
  • D6 Complete Basic Level & Vertical Slice Design — one level, whole experience: win, lose, retry.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: A complete basic level authored in the team's editor, with success and failure conditions and easy retry/continue; the vertical-slice experience arc (or an approved waiver for endless/no-fail designs).
    • Prerequisites: M1 D6 (level design) or D1 ⭐; CS M2 G2 ⭐ (editor).
    • Feeds into: The showcase demo; T2 full-level production.
    • Quality ladder: Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard). Minimum Expectation = paced, teaching, polished. Exceptional = a true vertical slice — all mechanics represented with intended experience quality.
  • D7 Systems Balance & Tuning — Data-Driven — turning the M1 model into a tuned game via the team's own data files.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: Tuning passes on the real build using the CS data-driven architecture (no code changes), tuning journal (what/why/result), final balance state for the showcase.
    • Prerequisites: M1 D7 (model); CS M1 G2 ⭐ (data files to tune).
    • Feeds into: Showcase play quality; M2 D5 feeds it data.
    • Quality ladder: Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard). Minimum Expectation = playtest-informed decisions. Exceptional = telemetry-assisted tuning.
  • D8 Game Feel & Feedback Pass ("Juice") — every action answers the player: visually, audibly, immediately.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: Feedback spec + implementation with CS: hit reactions, particles, screen effects, audio cues on meaningful interactions (objects, NPCs, items) serving the core mechanics.
    • Prerequisites: M1 D5 (feel spec); CS graphics/animation goals.
    • Feeds into: M2 D1 ⭐ and the showcase's perceived quality.
    • Quality ladder: Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard). Minimum Expectation = cues are meaningful and legible, not noise. Exceptional = the game feels professional — feedback is a signature.
  • D9 Narrative Presentation & Cutscenes — context delivered with motion: scrolling, parallax, zoom — never static walls of text.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: Primitive narrative cutscenes or ambient storytelling answering "who am I / where am I / what must I do", using dynamic presentation; integrated in the build.
    • Prerequisites: M1 D8 (beats); art assets from Art track or M1 D11.
    • Feeds into: Showcase storytelling; T2 full narrative.
    • Quality ladder: Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard). Minimum Expectation = dynamic presentation (parallax/zoom/scroll). Exceptional = presentation quality that elevates the whole product.
  • D10 — moved: now U5 Mobile UX Adaptation & Ergonomics in the UI/UX Goals Pool.
  • D11 Art Implementation Quality (conditional — mandatory pick for teams with NO Art student) — the design-owned substitute for the Art track's M2.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: All implemented art aligned with the Art POC (no placeholders): readable environments, critical sprites/animations for key actions, consistent scaling (matching pixel sizes, no distortion), UI states implemented; all shipped art team-made.
    • Prerequisites: M1 D11 (Art POC); CS art pipeline goals.
    • Feeds into: Showcase visual quality; T2 art production plan.
    • Quality ladder: Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard). Minimum Expectation = environment alive (layering, ambient animation). Exceptional = near-industry quality throughout.

Category adaptations

Pattern approved by the Design instructor (Education, July 2026); the subsections follow the approved model. The pool above is canonical and Games-flavored; most picks are universal design disciplines and apply to every category as-is (user research D3, playtests D9; branding M2 D2, playtesting program M2 D5, and both conditionals). The UI/UX universals — flows & wireframes, accessibility, motion design, onboarding, mobile ergonomics — moved to the UI/UX Goals Pool (July 26 split) and apply per its own Category calibrations. The subsections below give only the category readings for D1 ⭐ and the picks whose meaning shifts. Unlisted picks apply unchanged.

Education + Training

  • D1 ⭐ (M1) Features Design Document — for this category it covers: learning objectives and target learners replace genre/audience; the problem statement is the skill gap the product closes; feasibility unchanged. D1 ⭐ (M2): UX validation with real learners where possible; final polished UI unchanged.
  • M1 D2 Core-Loop Interactive Prototype → prototype of the core exercise loop (one drill/exercise playable end to end: attempt → feedback → next).
  • M1 D5 Control Mapping & Game Feel Specification → Interaction & Feedback Specification: input mapping for drills (touch/keyboard), feedback timing rules (correct/incorrect within 100 ms), error-tolerance conventions.
  • M1 D6 Level & Progression Design → Curriculum & Difficulty Progression: lesson sequencing, difficulty curves, mastery gates, remediation paths.
  • M1 D7 Game Economy & Systems Balance Model → Scoring, Streaks & Motivation Systems: points, streaks, spaced-repetition parameters, reward cadence — tuned against the learning goals, not against engagement alone.
  • M1 D8 Narrative, Theme & World Concept → Content Theme & Scenario Design: story wrappers for lessons, scenario design (e.g. driving-theory situations), age-appropriate tone.
  • M2 D6 Complete Basic Level & Vertical Slice → one complete lesson unit polished end to end (intro → exercises → assessment → results).
  • M2 D7 Systems Balance & Tuning → difficulty-curve tuning from playtest/telemetry data (error rates per exercise drive re-sequencing).
  • M2 D8 Game Feel & Feedback Pass → feedback & celebration pass: correct/incorrect/progress moments feel rewarding without distracting from learning.
  • M2 D9 Narrative Presentation & Cutscenes → guided sequences & worked examples: intro sequences, demonstration replays, tutor walk-throughs.

Simulator

  • D1 ⭐ (M1) Features Design Document — for this category it covers: the modeled system, who consumes the insights and for what decisions, fidelity-vs-usability trade-offs, feasibility. D1 ⭐ (M2): UX validation with users interpreting results; final polished UI unchanged.
  • M1 D2 Prototype → the configure → run → read-results loop, interactive end to end.
  • M1 D5 Control Mapping & Game Feel → Run-Control & Visualization Spec: play/pause/speed/step controls, camera & inspection interactions, live-view readability rules.
  • M1 D6 Level & Progression → Scenario Set Design: authored scenarios from baseline to stress and edge cases, ordered for demos.
  • M1 D7 Economy & Balance → Parameter Model Design: parameter ranges, defaults and plausibility bounds, designed with the CS team.
  • M1 D8 Narrative/Theme → Case-Study Framing: the real-world story each scenario tells (a city, an ecosystem), naming and visual metaphor.
  • M2 D6 Vertical Slice → one polished experiment end to end (configure → run → batch → report).
  • M2 D7 Balance & Tuning → parameter tuning against reference data or documented expectations.
  • M2 D8 Feedback Pass → legibility pass on the live view: transitions, highlights, alerts that make the simulation satisfying to watch.
  • M2 D9 Cutscenes → guided tours / annotated replays of notable runs.

Fintech

  • D1 ⭐ (M1) Features Design Document — for this category it covers: user goals (learn / monitor / decide), trust and clarity requirements, virtual-money disclaimers, feasibility. D1 ⭐ (M2) unchanged.
  • M1 D2 Prototype → the core financial loop (observe → decide → act → review outcome).
  • M1 D5 Control Mapping & Game Feel → Interaction & Data-Density Spec: chart interactions, order/entry ergonomics, error prevention for money-like actions.
  • M1 D6 Level & Progression → User Journey & Scenario Design: onboarding to first action; scripted market scenarios (calm, volatile, crash).
  • M1 D7 Economy & Balance → Economy & Risk Model Design: virtual portfolio rules, fees, limits — with the CS simulation.
  • M1 D8 Narrative/Theme → Product Voice & Scenario Theming: sober vs playful tone, market narratives for demos.
  • M2 D6 Vertical Slice → one complete workflow polished (e.g. fund → trade → report).
  • M2 D7 Balance & Tuning → tuning fees/volatility/difficulty from playtests.
  • M2 D8 Feedback Pass → clarity pass: confirmations, alerts, P&L moments — no dark patterns.
  • M2 D9 Cutscenes → guided walkthroughs / annotated market replays.

GovTech

  • D1 ⭐ (M1) Features Design Document — for this category it covers: the service blueprint across the citizen/customer side and the staff/ops side, stakeholders, accessibility mandate, feasibility. D1 ⭐ (M2) unchanged.
  • M1 D2 Prototype → the service flow (submit → track → resolve), both roles clickable.
  • M1 D5 Control Mapping & Game Feel → Form & Status Ergonomics Spec: form patterns, validation behaviour, status feedback conventions.
  • M1 D6 Level & Progression → Service Journey Design: end-to-end citizen journeys incl. rejection/appeal paths; staff workload paths.
  • M1 D7 Economy & Balance → Rules & Priority Model Design: queue priorities, SLA thresholds, escalation rules — with the CS engine.
  • M1 D8 Narrative/Theme → Civic Identity & Plain Language: trust cues, multilingual tone, content standards.
  • M2 D6 Vertical Slice → one service polished end to end (citizen submits → staff resolves → citizen notified).
  • M2 D7 Balance & Tuning → threshold/priority tuning under simulated load.
  • M2 D8 Feedback Pass → status & notification pass: clarity and reassurance at every state change.
  • M2 D9 Cutscenes → role onboarding walkthroughs and the showcase demo storyline.

Social Media

  • D1 ⭐ (M1) Features Design Document — for this category it covers: the target community, interaction norms, safety-by-design, feasibility. D1 ⭐ (M2) unchanged.
  • M1 D2 Prototype → the core social loop (post → react → notify → return).
  • M1 D5 Control Mapping & Game Feel → Feed & Composer Ergonomics Spec: gestures, composer flow, notification behaviour.
  • M1 D6 Level & Progression → Community Journey Design: new-user journey, empty states, seeding strategy for believable demos.
  • M1 D7 Economy & Balance → Engagement & Reputation Model: points/karma/limits balanced against abuse — with the moderation pipeline.
  • M1 D8 Narrative/Theme → Community Identity & Tone: naming, voice, presentation of guidelines.
  • M2 D6 Vertical Slice → one community flow polished (e.g. create event → invite → attend → recap).
  • M2 D7 Balance & Tuning → ranking/limit tuning from usage tests.
  • M2 D8 Feedback Pass → reactions, streaks and presence moments that reward participation.
  • M2 D9 Cutscenes → onboarding stories over seeded community content.

CMS

  • D1 ⭐ (M1) Features Design Document — for this category it covers: author and audience personas, content-model concepts, WYSIWYG expectations, feasibility. D1 ⭐ (M2) unchanged.
  • M1 D2 Prototype → the authoring loop (create → edit → preview → publish).
  • M1 D5 Control Mapping & Game Feel → Editor Interaction Spec: selection, drag-drop, inline editing conventions, undo expectations.
  • M1 D6 Level & Progression → Template & Content Structure Design: page archetypes, block taxonomy, starter templates.
  • M1 D7 Economy & Balance → Publishing Workflow Design: draft/review/publish states, roles, versioning UX.
  • M1 D8 Narrative/Theme → Theme & Demo Content Strategy: the demo site's content story, theme direction.
  • M2 D6 Vertical Slice → one authoring-to-published-site journey polished with real content.
  • M2 D7 Balance & Tuning → workflow tuning from author tests (friction points, defaults).
  • M2 D8 Feedback Pass → editor feel pass: micro-feedback, drag ghosting, save states.
  • M2 D9 Cutscenes → guided editor tours and template showcase sequences.

Immersive Experience

  • D1 ⭐ (M1) Features Design Document — for this category it covers: theme, emotional arc, context/space of use, feasibility. D1 ⭐ (M2) unchanged.
  • M1 D2 Prototype → one interactive scene/moment working end to end.
  • M1 D6 Level & Progression → Experience Flow & Pacing: scene sequence, dwell-time design, transitions.
  • M1 D7 Economy & Balance → Reactivity Model: mapping inputs (audio, motion, touch) to response intensities.
  • M2 D6 Vertical Slice → one complete scene/chapter polished.
  • M2 D7 Balance & Tuning → reactivity tuning (thresholds, easing) from observing visitors.

Creative Tools & Media Production

  • D1 ⭐ (M1) Features Design Document — for this category it covers: target creators, core workflows, competitive tool analysis, feasibility. D1 ⭐ (M2): UX validation with real creators; final polished UI unchanged.
  • M1 D2 Prototype → the core creation workflow (create → edit → export happy path).
  • M1 D5 Control Mapping & Game Feel → Tool Interaction Spec: shortcuts, modes, direct-manipulation conventions, touch adaptation.
  • M1 D6 Level & Progression → Workflow & Learning-Curve Design: novice→power-user progression, defaults, preset ladder.
  • M1 D7 Economy & Balance → Preset & Constraint Model: sensible parameter ranges, starter content balance.
  • M1 D8 Narrative/Theme → Product Identity & Showcase Content Plan: the tool's voice and what gets made with it for the demo.
  • M2 D6 Vertical Slice → one real artifact produced end to end in the tool, polished.
  • M2 D7 Balance & Tuning → ergonomics tuning from creator tests (click counts, friction).
  • M2 D8 Feedback Pass → tool feel pass: snapping, ghosting, micro-animations, satisfying manipulation.
  • M2 D9 Cutscenes → tutorial sequences and made-with-it showcase reels.

Visualization & Analytics

  • D1 ⭐ (M1) Features Design Document — for this category it covers: who reads this data and for what decisions, the explore/monitor loops, trust and legibility requirements, feasibility. D1 ⭐ (M2) unchanged.
  • M1 D2 Prototype → the core explore loop (load/see data → drill down → act or export).
  • M1 D5 Control Mapping & Game Feel → Interaction & Data-Density Spec: pan/zoom/brush conventions, drill-down patterns, legibility rules for dense views.
  • M1 D6 Level & Progression → View & Drill-Down Architecture: overview → detail journeys, default views per persona.
  • M1 D7 Game Economy & Balance → Metric & Threshold Design: which metrics matter, sensible defaults, alert thresholds designed with users.
  • M1 D8 Narrative/Theme → Data Storytelling: how views narrate (annotations, highlights, guided reading order).
  • M2 D6 Vertical Slice → one question answered end to end (raw data → explored → shared/exported view).
  • M2 D7 Balance & Tuning → defaults/threshold tuning from real usage.
  • M2 D8 Feedback Pass → clarity pass — density without noise, alert moments unmistakable, no chart junk.
  • M2 D9 Cutscenes → guided tours / annotated walkthroughs of key views.
