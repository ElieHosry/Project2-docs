Art Goals Pool

Status: approved by the Art instructor (July 2026). Distilled from the previous module's Art-Rubrics (M1–M3) and the New Preliminary Design timeline. The base pool below is Games-flavored; the Category adaptations section covers all 10 categories. Companion to the Design Goals Pool and the Team Formation Guide.

How Art goals work

Each team has 0–2 Art students (headcount confirmed at team formation, justified by the project's art surface). Like the Design track: each Art student takes 3 deliverable-sized goals per milestone (≈ 1.5 CS goals in effort; raised from 2 in July 2026 so the Art pool stays reachable). The first artist owns the common goal A1 ⭐ plus 2 pool picks; a second artist takes 3 more picks (all picks with instructor approval).

Teams with no Art student do not use this pool — the Design track's conditional goals (Design M1 D11 Art Proof of Concept, M2 D11 Art Implementation Quality) cover the minimum art surface instead.

Sizing and grading: same model as Design — goals are sized by deliverable + quality tier, each carrying a Quality ladder (Exceptional → Good → Expectation → Minimum → Fail) distilled from the old rubric tiers. Rungs map directly to the Score dropdown — reaching a rung earns that score, not the deadline. Score scale: Untested / Exceptional / Good / Expectation / Minimum / Fail (graded by the Art instructor; Expectation is the pass floor).

AI policy (from the old rubrics): AI may be used for ideation and reference only — every AI-generated image must be annotated with the tool used; AI-generated assets may NOT ship in the prototype or product. All shipped art is team-made.

Cross-track dependencies: Art consumes the Design track's theme/Features Design Document (D1 ⭐) and style decisions, and delivers through the CS art pipeline (data-driven asset loading, spritesheets, ≤4k texture resolution). The art document (working file in PowerPoint, submitted as PDF) is a living deliverable across both milestones — updates and new work must be clearly indicated each milestone.

Numbering: A1 ⭐ = common; A2, A3, … = pool picks. Pools are extensible — instructor-approved goals from new proposals are appended at the end.

Art — Games

Milestone 1 (Proof of Concept)

  • M1A1 ⭐ Art Bible / Proof of Concept — the game's complete visual direction in one professional document.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: Art POC document (pptx working file → PDF): content page, moodboards, art direction and style guide, write-ups for game concept, characters, environments, props, UI and VFX (as applicable), and at least 2 in-game mockup screens (typically main menu + in-game) that clearly indicate art direction and gameplay.
    • Prerequisites: Design M1D1 ⭐ (theme, genre, core concept).
    • Feeds into: Every other Art goal; Design M2D2 (branding); CS art pipeline goals; the M1 "first-pass art doc" checkpoint (wk9).
    • Quality ladder: Exceptional = professionally presented, high-quality assets, strong visual appeal throughout. Good = solidly above the Expectation bar, approaching Exceptional. Expectation = additional mockup screens, clear descriptions, consistent and aesthetically coherent direction. Minimum = partial progress, clearly below the Expectation bar. Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard).
  • M1A2 Character Concepts & Model Sheets — the cast designed, researched and ready for production.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: Research and conceptual designs for characters; main character + at least 1 enemy or NPC completed with solid anatomy and draftsmanship; model sheets and character studies (key poses, expressions).
    • Prerequisites: M1A1 ⭐ (art direction), Design M1D8 (world/character bible) if picked.
    • Feeds into: M2A2 (animation), M2A1 ⭐ (production); the design prototype's placeholder replacement.
    • Quality ladder: Exceptional = professional draftsmanship and consistency across the cast. Good = solidly above the Expectation bar, approaching Exceptional. Expectation = appealing, style-consistent designs with model sheets and studies. Minimum = partial progress, clearly below the Expectation bar. Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard).
  • M1A3 Environment, Props & VFX Concepts — where the game lives: key environments, props and effect designs.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: Research and concept designs for environments, props and VFX (as applicable); key environment clearly designed, rendered and presented with good draftsmanship.
    • Prerequisites: M1A1 ⭐ (art direction).
    • Feeds into: M2A3 (living environment), M2A1 ⭐ (production), Design M1D6 (level design visuals).
    • Quality ladder: Exceptional = professional-quality environment art, props and VFX concepts. Good = solidly above the Expectation bar, approaching Exceptional. Expectation = additional environments; direction refined through thorough research; blends well with characters and UI. Minimum = partial progress, clearly below the Expectation bar. Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard).
  • M1A4 UI Art Concepts & Typography — the interface's visual identity: type, logo, HUD clarity.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: UI concept designs and research; typography and logo exploration; brand identity of team and game represented; HUD elements designed for clarity; all UI art consistent with the game's overall direction.
    • Prerequisites: M1A1 ⭐ (style guide), Design M1D4 (wireframes to skin).
    • Feeds into: M2A4 (UI states), Design M2D1 ⭐ (final polished UI), Design M2D2 (branding).
    • Quality ladder: Exceptional = appealing, high-quality designs that fit the game. Good = solidly above the Expectation bar, approaching Exceptional. Expectation = designs synergize with game concepts; typography/logo/brand identity explored; HUD clarity. Minimum = partial progress, clearly below the Expectation bar. Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard).
  • M1A5 Animation Foundations — timing, key poses and the first cycles that prove the characters can move.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: Animation plan for critical gameplay actions; key poses and timing tests; at least the main character's core cycle (e.g., idle/walk) demonstrating understanding of animation principles.
    • Prerequisites: M1A2 (character designs) or M1A1 ⭐.
    • Feeds into: M2A2 (extensive animation), CS animation system (spritesheet format).
    • Quality ladder: Exceptional = strong command of animation principles across tests. Good = solidly above the Expectation bar, approaching Exceptional. Expectation = cycles show character personality, not generic motion. Minimum = partial progress, clearly below the Expectation bar. Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard).
  • M1A6 Early Asset Production — Prototype Support — first-pass critical assets flowing into the prototype from week 8.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: First-pass critical sprites/tiles/UI elements delivered for the design prototype (production starts by wk8 per the module timeline), integrated at correct scale, consistent with the art bible.
    • Prerequisites: M1A1 ⭐ (direction), Design M1D2 (the prototype to support).
    • Feeds into: M2A1 ⭐ (production continues), the wk9 prototype checkpoint's visual credibility.
    • Quality ladder: Exceptional = near-final quality already in the prototype. Good = solidly above the Expectation bar, approaching Exceptional. Expectation = consistent style, correct scaling, readable gameplay. Minimum = partial progress, clearly below the Expectation bar. Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard).

Milestone 2 (Art Document)

  • M2A1 ⭐ Critical Asset Production & In-Engine Visual Quality — every critical asset completed, implemented and consistent in the showcase build.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: All critical art (characters, environments, props, UI) completed at minimum first-pass quality and implemented in the showcase build — no placeholders; correct scale (matching pixel sizes, no stretching/distortion); consistent art direction; the art document updated with all M2 work clearly indicated (and reasons noted if any asset is not yet in the engine).
    • Prerequisites: All M1 Art goals taken; CS art pipeline (data-driven asset loading).
    • Feeds into: The trimester showcase; sets the visual standard the T2 product must match; T2 asset production plan.
    • Quality ladder: Exceptional = high-quality art throughout, professional attention to detail. Good = solidly above the Expectation bar, approaching Exceptional. Expectation = good draftsmanship, well-themed, strong consistency across all assets. Minimum = partial progress, clearly below the Expectation bar. Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard).
  • M2A2 Character Animation — Extensive — the cast in motion: complete key animations, personality, breadth.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: Key character/prop animations completed (main character + 1 enemy/NPC minimum) supporting all critical gameplay actions; player character animation extensive and lively, showing clear personality; implemented in the build.
    • Prerequisites: M1A5 (foundations), M1A2 (designs); CS animation system.
    • Feeds into: M2A1 ⭐ (implementation quality), Design M2D8 (game feel).
    • Quality ladder: Exceptional = high quality with strong animation principles; additional animation sets for other characters. Good = solidly above the Expectation bar, approaching Exceptional. Expectation = decent quality throughout; personality evident. Minimum = partial progress, clearly below the Expectation bar. Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard).
  • M2A3 Environment Alive — Ambient Animation & Layering — depth, light and motion that make the world breathe.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: Environment implemented with foreground/background layering for depth, lighting mood, and ambient animation (moving clouds, swaying trees, water, dust, blinking lights, creatures).
    • Prerequisites: M1A3 (environment concepts); CS rendering (layers).
    • Feeds into: M2A1 ⭐; the showcase's perceived production value.
    • Quality ladder: Exceptional = a living world — environment art as a signature of the game. Good = solidly above the Expectation bar, approaching Exceptional. Expectation = several ambient animations create life. Minimum = partial progress, clearly below the Expectation bar. Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard).
  • M2A4 UI Art — States & Feedback — complete interface art with living states, policy-compliant type and themed menus.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: All critical UI (menus, HUD) art complete — no WIP or placeholders; fonts appropriate and policy-compliant (max 3: headings, in-game, small text); text clean, readable, well-aligned; visual feedback states (press/hover/on-off, glows, size changes) implemented; menus and HUD themed to the game.
    • Prerequisites: M1A4 (UI concepts), Design M1D4 (flows); CS GUI capability.
    • Feeds into: Design M2D1 ⭐ (final polished UI), Design M2D3 (motion design).
    • Quality ladder: Exceptional = high-quality themed menus/HUD with animated flair. Good = solidly above the Expectation bar, approaching Exceptional. Expectation = feedback states implemented; clarity and intuitive navigation. Minimum = partial progress, clearly below the Expectation bar. Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard).
  • M2A5 VFX — Critical Effects & Transitions — the effects that sell gameplay moments, plus transitions with style.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: All VFX critical to gameplay at first-pass quality (impacts, combat moments, pickups, respawns), of reasonable artistic quality without glitches; menu/HUD VFX considered.
    • Prerequisites: M1A3 (VFX concepts); CS particle/graphics goals.
    • Feeds into: Design M2D8 (juice pass), M2A1 ⭐.
    • Quality ladder: Exceptional = interesting VFX transitions (levels, respawns, impacts) — Persona 5-style motion identity. Good = solidly above the Expectation bar, approaching Exceptional. Expectation = high-quality VFX showing animation principles. Minimum = partial progress, clearly below the Expectation bar. Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard).
  • M2A6 Cutscenes & Storyboards — the narrative visualized: from thumbnails to rendered scenes.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: Cutscene planning and production: research, thumbnail sketches, storyboards, color scripts, progressing to rendered first-pass cutscene art for the scenes the game needs (waivable if the game has no cutscenes).
    • Prerequisites: Design M1D8 (narrative beats), M1A2 (characters).
    • Feeds into: Design M2D9 (narrative presentation), the showcase's storytelling.
    • Quality ladder: Exceptional = all cutscene art rendered at first-pass quality. Good = solidly above the Expectation bar, approaching Exceptional. Expectation = partial scenes rendered. Minimum = partial progress, clearly below the Expectation bar. Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard).
  • M2A7 Additional Asset Expansion — beyond the critical set: more levels, characters, props, UI variants.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: A defined expansion set beyond the critical assets — additional level/segment art, NPCs, props, UI variants or VFX — with research/concepts where new ground is broken; only attempted once critical assets are complete.
    • Prerequisites: M2A1 ⭐ well underway (critical set first).
    • Feeds into: T2 production head start; showcase breadth.
    • Quality ladder: Exceptional = significant quantity at high quality. Good = solidly above the Expectation bar, approaching Exceptional. Expectation = good draftsmanship and consistency throughout. Minimum = partial progress, clearly below the Expectation bar. Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard).

Category adaptations

Pattern approved by the Art instructor (Education, July 2026); the subsections follow the approved model. Each subsection below gives a complete per-goal reading for every Art goal (M1 A1–A6, M2 A1–A7): a category-specific deliverable where the goal is category-specific, a de-Gamed reading where the discipline is universal, and an explicit N/A where the category's art surface doesn't need that goal (game-native goals like character animation or cutscenes rarely apply to utility products). Each subsection also carries surface & headcount guidance (the team-formation rule: the art surface justifies the slot). The base pool above stays Games-flavored — it is the live Games pool; these readings are what the other 9 categories use.

Education + Training

  • Surface & headcount: mid-to-high for visual learning products (mascots, exercise illustrations, scene art) — 1 artist typical, 2 justified for character/animation-heavy learning games; 0 acceptable for text/audio-centric trainers (typing tutor, ear training), covered by the Design conditionals.
  • M1 A1 ⭐ Art Bible / POC → visual language of the learning product: age-appropriate style, mascot/instructor direction, iconography; the 2 mockup screens = 2 exercise screens.
  • M1 A2 Character Concepts → mascot / instructor character concepts & model sheets (expression sheet: neutral, encouraging, corrective).
  • M1 A3 Environment, Props & VFX Concepts → exercise-scene & item illustration concepts (the objects lessons manipulate) plus feedback-VFX concepts.
  • M1 A4 UI Art Concepts & Typography → learner-UI art: icon set, progress/streak visuals, large readable type for young learners.
  • M1 A5 Animation Foundations → feedback & mascot animation (correct/incorrect reactions, idle life); timing tests on the mascot's core reactions.
  • M1 A6 Early Asset Production → first-pass exercise / mascot / UI assets delivered into the M1 lesson prototype.
  • M2 A1 ⭐ Critical Asset Production → exercise scenes, mascot set and feedback visuals completed and in-engine for the showcase lesson unit.
  • M2 A2 Character Animation → mascot reaction/animation set integrated with the Design feedback pass (personality across correct/incorrect/idle).
  • M2 A3 Environment Alive → lively exercise scenes — ambient motion that adds life without distracting from the task.
  • M2 A4 UI Art — States & Feedback → learner-UI art states: button/press/hover, progress bars, streak/level visuals, themed and readable.
  • M2 A5 VFX → celebration & progress effects (streaks, level-ups, completion), reasonable quality without glitches.
  • M2 A6 Cutscenes & Storyboards → tutorial / worked-example story sequences framing lesson units; waivable if the product has none.
  • M2 A7 Additional Asset Expansion → extra scenes, mascot variants and item sets once the critical assets are complete.

Simulator

  • Surface & headcount: low-to-mid (world stylization, agent sprites, data-viz styling) — 0–1 artist; 0 common (Design conditionals cover it). 1 justified for stylized, watchable sims.
  • M1 A1 ⭐ Art Bible / POC → visual language of the simulated world + dashboard/report styling; mockups = live view + report screen.
  • M1 A2 Character Concepts → agent / vehicle / entity sprite concepts & variants (the moving units the sim renders).
  • M1 A3 Environment, Props & VFX Concepts → world/environment concepts (tiles, maps, zone styling) plus event/alert VFX concepts.
  • M1 A4 UI Art Concepts & Typography → run-control & data-viz UI art: control iconography, chart/graph styling, legible dense-view typography.
  • M1 A5 Animation Foundations → agent motion & state-transition foundations (movement cycles, state changes).
  • M1 A6 Early Asset Production → first-pass world / agent / UI assets into the M1 sim prototype.
  • M2 A1 ⭐ Critical Asset Production → world tiles, agent sprites and dashboard styling completed and in-engine.
  • M2 A2 Character Animation → agent animation sets (movement, state changes) implemented in the build.
  • M2 A3 Environment Alive → living-world ambience (ambient motion, weather/light) that makes the sim watchable.
  • M2 A4 UI Art — States & Feedback → run-control & dashboard UI art states: control feedback, chart styling, alert visuals.
  • M2 A5 VFX → state-change VFX — alerts, events, thresholds crossed.
  • M2 A6 Cutscenes & Storyboards → N/A — sims have no cutscenes; supply intro/tour storyboards only if the showcase needs a guided walkthrough.
  • M2 A7 Additional Asset Expansion → extra agent types, map/zone art and scenario styling once critical assets are done.

Fintech

  • Surface & headcount: low — 0–1 artist; 0 typical. 1 justified only for brand/illustration-led products.
  • M1 A1 ⭐ Art Bible / POC → brand-led visual language + data-viz styling; mockups = dashboard + a mobile action flow.
  • M1 A2 Character Concepts → N/A for most — an optional mascot / empty-state illustration system only if the product is illustration-led.
  • M1 A3 Environment, Props & VFX Concepts → iconography & illustration concepts (categories, empty states, product imagery) plus subtle state-VFX concepts.
  • M1 A4 UI Art Concepts & Typography → financial UI art: chart/table styling, numeric legibility, trust cues, icon set.
  • M1 A5 Animation Foundations → micro-animation foundations (number tickers, chart transitions, confirmations).
  • M1 A6 Early Asset Production → first-pass icon / illustration / UI assets into the M1 prototype.
  • M2 A1 ⭐ Critical Asset Production → brand system, iconography and chart/dashboard styling completed and in-engine.
  • M2 A2 Character Animation → N/A unless illustration-led — then an animated illustration / empty-state set.
  • M2 A3 Environment Alive → subtle dashboard ambience — restrained motion appropriate to a money product.
  • M2 A4 UI Art — States & Feedback → financial UI art states: confirmations, alerts, P&L moments; clean and trustworthy, no dark patterns.
  • M2 A5 VFX → transaction / alert effects — success/failure/threshold feedback, tasteful.
  • M2 A6 Cutscenes & Storyboards → N/A — no cutscenes; brief onboarding storyboards only if used.
  • M2 A7 Additional Asset Expansion → extra icon sets, illustration variants and chart types once critical assets are done.

  • M1 A4 UI Art Concepts & Typography → form & status UI art: accessible iconography, status colours, legible civic typography.
  • M1 A5 Animation Foundations → micro-animation foundations (status transitions, submit/confirm feedback) — restrained and accessible.
  • M1 A6 Early Asset Production → first-pass icon / illustration / UI assets into the M1 service prototype.
  • M2 A1 ⭐ Critical Asset Production → accessible icon/illustration set, map styling and status visuals completed and in-engine.
  • M2 A2 Character Animation → N/A — no character animation; animated service illustrations only if illustration-led.
  • M2 A3 Environment Alive → N/A — no living environment; subtle map/status ambience only where it aids clarity.
  • M2 A4 UI Art — States & Feedback → form & status UI art states: clear submit/track/resolve feedback, accessible contrast.
  • M2 A5 VFX → status-change effects — submission accepted, in-progress, resolved; reassuring, not flashy.
  • M2 A6 Cutscenes & Storyboards → explainer / role-onboarding storyboards (how to submit; how staff resolve); waivable.
  • M2 A7 Additional Asset Expansion → extra service icon sets, illustration variants and map styles once critical assets are done.
