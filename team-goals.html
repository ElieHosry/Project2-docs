TEAM Goals — Audio & Final Presentation

Status: Audio goals revised by the Audio instructor (July 2026); Final Presentation approved July 2026. The TEAM track carries the module's cross-disciplinary common goals — Audio (two goals, carried at both milestones) and the Final Presentation (M2 only). Companion to the CS goal pools, the Design Goals Pool, the UI/UX Goals Pool, the Art Goals Pool and the Team Formation Guide.

What changed in the July 2026 Audio revision:

  • The single TA1 ⭐ Audio Direction & Plan → Audio Integration & Quality structure is replaced by two audio goals carried at both milestones: TA1 ⭐ Overall Audio Experience and TA2 ⭐ Audio Engine Functionality.
  • The old M1 Audio Concept Document is no longer a milestone goal — it is delivered at the week-4 project pitch (see the Team Formation Guide and the Proposal Assessor Guide).
  • Assessment is progress-based: deliverables are judged against the project's actual build state at each milestone.
  • BGM and SFX are the default in every category — there are no BGM-optional categories; any exception follows the waiver process below.
  • Audio-engine functionality is now graded by the Audio instructor via TA2 ⭐ — judged on usability and functional behaviour in the engine demo itself — rather than as a separately-scored CS goal. The CS goals that build the audio engine remain in the pools (they are the engineering vehicle, especially where audio/DSP is the product's core: the rhythm game, the audio editor / mini-DAW, the music tracker, the ear-training app, and the generative / spatial-audio experiences). TA2 grades the resulting audio system's behaviour for every team, so audio quality is guaranteed even in teams that would not otherwise foreground it.

The two audio goals are uniform across all 10 categories; the All-categories calibrations section sets the per-category M2 floor.

How TEAM goals work

TEAM goals are cross-disciplinary goals owned by the whole team. Every team carries TA1 ⭐ and TA2 ⭐ (Audio) in each milestone, plus TP1 ⭐ (Final Presentation) in M2 — all mandatory (only the lecturer can waive an audio requirement, explicitly and on the record, e.g. for a deliberately soundless product or a project with no functional build yet; TP1 ⭐ is never waived).

Champion: the team names one champion per TEAM goal (any track) who coordinates it. Coordination happens inside the champion's existing goals' scope; the underlying work is distributed across track goals — the CS audio-engine work, Design's audio direction, Art's SFX-supporting visuals.

Grading: the old module's rule is preserved — a TEAM goal's grade affects every member. TA1 ⭐ and TA2 ⭐ are graded by the Audio instructor (floating across teams); TP1 ⭐ is graded jointly by the attending instructors at the end-of-trimester session. Scale for all three: Fail / Minimum Expectation / Exceptional (see below).

Numbering & extensibility: TA1 ⭐ / TA2 ⭐ (Audio) and TP1 ⭐ (Presentation) are the mandatory TEAM goals. The pools are extensible — future instructor-approved goals would join as TA3 / TP2, … optional picks.

Core policy: BGM & SFX are the default

Every project, in every category, carries BGM and the SFX its category needs by default. There are no BGM-optional categories.

If a team believes its project does not need BGM or SFX (e.g. a deliberately soundless product), this is handled at the lecturer's discretion, case by case, only after the team has discussed it with the lecturer. A waiver is never assumed — it must be explicitly granted and recorded.

Progress-based expectations: deliverables are assessed relative to the project's actual progress at each milestone. If a project does not yet have a functional build, it is at the lecturer's discretion to waive audio requirements on an individual basis.

The two audio goals

Every team carries the same two audio goals at both milestones:

  • TA1 ⭐ Overall Audio Experience — the audio content and how well it serves the project.
  • TA2 ⭐ Audio Engine Functionality — the engine capability that plays, manages and (where needed) processes that audio.

Assessment criteria

These criteria drive grading at both milestones (scaled to the project's stage per the progress-based policy).

TA1 ⭐ Overall Audio Experience — Considering the current stage of development, how effectively does the audio contribute to the project? Are the audio assets of an appropriate quality, edited and prepared appropriately for use within the project? Do they suit the style, purpose and intended experience of the project, with sufficient audio included to effectively support the user experience? Has the existing audio been implemented correctly, with sounds triggered appropriately and no noticeable audio bugs or implementation issues? Is appropriate progress being made towards delivering an effective final audio experience?

TA2 ⭐ Audio Engine Functionality — Where applicable, does the engine provide sufficient audio functionality to support the project's current audio requirements? Basic examples may include reliable playback and multi-channel support. Advanced examples may include spatial audio, real-time effects, or adaptive audio.

Milestone 1 deliverables (Research & early implementation phase)

Assessed against the early state of the project. Expectations follow the assessment criteria above, scaled to this stage.

  • TA1 ⭐ Overall Audio Experience
    Deliverables
    • Initial audio set in the prototype: BGM and SFX from the week-5 needs list, following the agreed priority order, present and working in the build.
    • Suitability: the audio used so far suits the style, purpose and intended experience of the project, with sufficient audio included to support the user experience at this point.
    • Asset quality: assets are of appropriate quality, edited and prepared appropriately for use within the project.
    • Implementation: existing audio is implemented correctly where applicable; sounds are triggered appropriately, with no noticeable audio bugs or implementation issues.
    • Quality ladder: Fail = below expectation — the assessment criteria are not met. Minimum Expectation = criteria somewhat met, needs improvement. Exceptional = criteria met completely, close to industry standard.
  • TA2 ⭐ Audio Engine Functionality
    Deliverables
    • Audio pipeline: new audio files can be added or modified without hard-coded file names or recompiling the code.
    • Editor support (where applicable): audio asset files can be added by drag and drop; loading an unsupported audio file format triggers clear error detection rather than a crash.
    • Playback: reliable playback with multi-channel support, so one sound does not cut off another.
    • Sync (where applicable): for projects whose concept depends on it (e.g. rhythm-based gameplay), audio, visuals and input are in sync and input is responsive.
    • Quality ladder: Fail = below expectation — the assessment criteria are not met. Minimum Expectation = criteria somewhat met, needs improvement. Exceptional = criteria met completely, close to industry standard.

Milestone 2 deliverables (Iterated Prototype phase — complete implementation based on the build)

Assessed against the showcase build. Expectations follow the assessment criteria above, at delivery standard.

  • TA1 ⭐ Overall Audio Experience
    Deliverables
    • Complete audio set based on the current build: BGM and SFX integrated in the showcase build; all audio fits the project's theme well, and all the SFX the project needs are present, with enough variation that they do not sound repetitive.
    • Quality: all audio is clean and well-edited; looping audio loops smoothly; audio assets are of high sonic quality.
    • Implementation: audio is triggered appropriately throughout the build, with no noticeable audio bugs or implementation issues; graded from the showcase build itself (a gameplay/interaction recording with good-quality audio and no narration).
    • Quality ladder: Fail = below expectation — the assessment criteria are not met. Minimum Expectation = criteria somewhat met, needs improvement. Exceptional = criteria met completely, close to industry standard (e.g. original BGM and/or SFX tailor-made by team members).
  • TA2 ⭐ Audio Engine Functionality
    Deliverables
    • Persistence: audio properties are de/serialised — saving and loading audio data per object or via an audio component.
    • Full functionality: the engine supports everything the project's final audio requires; reliable playback and multi-channel support as the baseline, with advanced functionality (spatial audio, real-time effects, adaptive audio) where the project calls for it.
    • Sync (where applicable): gameplay, and beat-mapping if applicable, makes sense rhythmically and stays in sync with audio and input.
    • Quality ladder: Fail = below expectation — the assessment criteria are not met. Minimum Expectation = criteria somewhat met, needs improvement. Exceptional = criteria met completely, close to industry standard.

Grading scale

Each audio goal is graded on a three-point scale, applied to every team member:

  • Fail — the assessment criteria are not met.
  • Minimum Expectation — the assessment criteria are somewhat met and need improvement.
  • Exceptional — the assessment criteria are met completely and close to industry standard.

Final Presentation

  • TP1 ⭐ Final Presentation (M2 only — uniform for all categories) — 7 minutes in front of the class and the instructors: what you built, how you built it, what you learned.
    Details & dependencies
    • Deliverable: A 7-minute team presentation at the end-of-trimester session (wk14, teams grouped by product genre): a live showcase of the project's custom tools (the editor/authoring pipeline), a live demo of the prototype on the showcase build, and a team post-mortem (what went well, what went wrong, what the team would do differently — across tracks). Hard 7-minute limit.
    • Prerequisites: The M2 showcase build; CS M2 G2 ⭐ (the tools to demo); every track's M2 outputs.
    • Feeds into: T2 planning (the post-mortem's actions); the module showcase.
    • Quality ladder: Fail = below expectation — goal not met (missing, incomplete, or not to standard). Minimum Expectation = rehearsed handoffs, every track represented, demo runs without stalls. Exceptional = a compelling narrative and an honest, actionable post-mortem that names causes, not symptoms.

All-categories calibrations (M2 floors)

BGM and SFX are the default in all categories; any exception follows the waiver process above. The floors below say what "suitable" means per category — the two audio goals themselves are identical everywhere.

  • Games / Immersive Experience — suitable BGM + SFX needed for the project; Immersive also meets the category's Spatial / Reactive Audio purple requirement, and the sync clause applies to audio-reactive pieces.
  • Education + Training — suitable BGM + SFX needed for the project; where feedback is pedagogical, distinct correct / incorrect / progress cues are the load-bearing ones. Products where audio is the content (ear training, rhythm exercises) treat TA1/TA2 as load-bearing and the sync clause applies.
  • Simulator — suitable BGM + SFX needed for the project.
  • Fintech — suitable BGM (BGM may not be needed) + SFX needed for the project.
  • GovTech — suitable BGM (BGM may not be needed) + SFX needed for the project; for accessibility, every audio cue should be paired with a visual equivalent.
  • Social Media — suitable BGM + SFX needed for the project; respectful defaults (mute available, no notification spam).
  • CMS — suitable BGM (BGM may not be needed) + SFX needed for the project; published sites are silent by default.
  • Creative Tools & Media Production — suitable BGM (BGM may not be needed) + SFX needed for the project; for audio products (DAW, tracker) TA1/TA2 are load-bearing and the sync clause applies.
  • Visualization & Analytics — suitable BGM (BGM may not be needed) + SFX needed for the project; as with GovTech, every audio cue should be paired with a visual equivalent.
